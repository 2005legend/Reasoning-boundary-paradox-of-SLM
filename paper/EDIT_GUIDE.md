# Paper master copy: edit guide

**Status (2026-10-04): complete 6-page IEEE draft. No real results yet.** Every result number
in `main.pdf` is a *pre-registered expectation*:
- Expected numbers are **red**, and a **PLACEHOLDER RESULTS** watermark is on every page.
- Sentences whose wording depends on the outcome are **violet**, tagged `[R1]`–`[R18]`.
- Open items that don't depend on results are **blue `[TODO: …]`**.

Do not quote any red number as a finding.

Paper: *Is Pass@k Shrinkage Real, and Do Its Fixes Compose? A Pre-Registered, Human-Audited Study in
Small Language Models.*

**The story in one breath.** The paper fills three different kinds of research gap. Keep all three visible
when editing; the evidence for each is in `../claude science/research_gaps_2026-10-05.md`.

| Gap | Type | What the paper does |
|---|---|---|
| Is shrinkage real? | **contradiction**: papers disagree. *Shrinks*: Yue, Nguyen, Wu & Xuan, Zhou & Li. *Expands*: ProRL, Wen et al. *Both*: Yao et al. *Measurement artifact*: Yuan et al., Dragoi et al. | a pre-registered multi-seed measurement at 0.5B with boundary entry/exit counts and rollouts per problem |
| What is lost? | **methodological**: everyone measures automatically; nobody asked people | a blinded human audit. H1: are the "modes" strategies? H2: were the lost problems ever solved by valid reasoning? |
| Do the fixes compose? | **novelty**: never tested | the 2×3 factorial (topic budget × {none, SELF, MEG}) with pre-registered contrasts |
The experiment it reports is `../heg_grpo_aws/` (code), `../heg_grpo_aws/PREREGISTRATION.md` (fixed design) and
`../claude science/novelty_verification_2026-10-03.md` (novelty and positioning).

## 4. After the AWS runs: updating every number takes one command

1. **On AWS**, once the main queue (and the extras, if run) has finished, run the `results` cell in
   `control.ipynb`. That is the same as running:
   `python analyze.py --model_size 0.5B --benchmark math500` (GSM8K was dropped, deviation 7).
   Then, **before terminating the instance**, run the "Human evaluation" cell. It builds the blinded sheets in
   `~/artifacts/human_eval/` from the saved completions.
2. **Copy the artifacts to the laptop** (`heg_grpo_aws/README.md` Part I):
   `scp -r heg:~/artifacts ./aws_artifacts`.
   - Give `aws_artifacts/human_eval/annotator_A` and `annotator_B` to two people, together with
     `heg_grpo_aws/HUMAN_EVAL_GUIDE.md`. That is about 4–5 hours each. Never give them `key.json`.
   - When both CSVs are back in place, run from `heg_grpo_aws/`:
     `python scripts/human_eval.py score --artifact_root ../aws_artifacts`.
     This writes `analysis/human_eval.json`.
3. **Fill the paper.** From `paper/`:
   ```
   python fill_numbers.py --artifacts ../aws_artifacts --gpu_hours <total A10G hours> --usd <total spend>
   ```
   - It reads `analysis/math500_0.5B.json`, `analysis/gsm8k_0.5B.json`, the run configs and logs, and
     `calibration/0.5B/calibration.json`.
   - It fills every table, all three figures, the abstract numbers, the calibration table, steps, learning
     rate, levels, partitioner, rollouts per problem, the crossover *k*, the SELF selection rate and the
     mode entropy.
   - Anything not found (for example the extras, if they weren't run) becomes `--`, and the script lists it.
   - Take `--gpu_hours` and `--usd` from AWS Billing → Cost Explorer; they are not in the logs.
4. **Recompile.** Numbers turn black; the watermark and draft box disappear.
5. **Do §5** (result-dependent wording) **and §6** (TODOs), then build with `\finaltrue`.

If the extras were not run: delete the two rows below the rule in Table IV, E2/E3 in Table V, and
sentences R10/R11 (or say "not run for budget reasons").

### Human-audit outcomes (rules from `PREREGISTRATION.md`, "Human evaluation")

**H1 (R17).**
- Balanced accuracy ≥ 70%: keep the current text.
- Below 70%: "…the partitioner reaches X% (balanced Y%), below the pre-registered 70% bar, so MEG's modes are
  surface-level clusters; we therefore read MEG as redistributing credit over surface variation, not over
  strategies." Then soften every place that calls modes "strategies" or "approaches": §III-C, R7, R10, R13,
  and the abstract (R17: "The human labels do not support…").

**H2 (R18, and the H2 part of R1, R2, R13, R14).**
- **Lucky hits.** Exited rate < 50% and significantly below retained (Fisher p < 0.05):
  - R18: "…only X% of the base model's successes on exited problems were valid reasoning, against Y% on matched
    retained problems (p = …): most of what Pass@32 counts as lost was never solved by valid reasoning."
  - R1/R2/R14: "measured shrinkage is mostly a loss of lucky hits". This supports the measurement-critique side
    of the debate [Dragoi, Taşaltı].
- **Genuine loss.** Exited rate ≥ 50% and not significantly lower:
  - R18: "…X% vs Y% (p = …): the problems vanilla GRPO loses were, as far as annotators can tell, solved by valid
    reasoning; the shrinkage is genuine."
  - R1/R2/R14: "shrinkage is genuine". This supports the shrinkage side [Yue, Nguyen].
- **Mixed** (the current text): anything else.

Also check that the §VI discussion (R13) cites the side of the debate the result supports.

### Templates for the other outcomes

These are the pre-registered reporting rules (`PREREGISTRATION.md`, "How each outcome will be reported").

**Outcome A: complementary.** P1 and/or P2 significant after Holm, bootstrap CI excluding 0.
- R1: "The composite of the topic budget and intra-group redistribution shrinks least, and both
  pre-registered primary contrasts favor it after Holm correction (P1 = …, P2 = …), indicating that the two
  levels act on distinct components of shrinkage." (If only one is significant, name that one and give the
  other as a bound.)
- R5: "Both primary contrasts clear the pre-registered bar (Table V): P1 = … (CI …, Holm p = …) and P2 = …
  (CI …, Holm p = …)."
- R13: "Shrinkage has two separable components: concentration within groups, which MEG addresses, and
  concentration of the gradient budget on a few topics, which CB addresses. Neither alone recovers what the
  composite does."
- R2 / R14: "...the two levels are complementary rather than redundant."
- Also re-check R8: if the composite helps, CB must actually be binding somewhere. Look at pos. weight and Fig. 3.

**Outcome B: bounded null.** This is the current text. Only refresh the numbers and the R-checks.

**Outcome C: redundant or negative.** P2 ≤ 0 (adding CB does not help) and/or E2 not significant (MEG is no
better than random partitions).
- R1: "Intra-group redistribution accounts for the reduction in shrinkage; adding the topic budget does not
  help (P2 = …, CI …), and the interaction is bounded within …"
- R10 if E2 ≈ 0: "Random mode labels give a similar slope (E2 = …, CI …), so the benefit comes from
  redistributing credit per se rather than from the partition." This weakens the "modes" story; say so in the
  limitations.

**Outcome D: no shrinkage.** Vanilla P@32 ≥ base, or the slope CI includes 0.
- R3: "At this budget, vanilla GRPO does not shrink the boundary on MATH-500: Pass@32 changes by … (slope …).
  With … rollouts per training problem, training may be below the regime in which shrinkage appears [12]."
- Keep all contrasts, as pre-registered. Reframe the abstract and conclusion around the bounds.

## 8. Decisions still open before the main runs (they change the paper)

1. **CB strength. DECIDED 2026-10-06 (after the pilot, before any main run).**
   - The pilot confirmed the problem: subject spend ratios were 0.38-1.46 after 30 steps, so the old
     θ = 1.5 never fired, and δ = 0.98 could not cut a weight below 0.90 anyway.
   - New rule (pre-registration deviation 6, §III-B of the paper): θ = 1.1, δ = 0.18, so a topic at 1.5× the
     mean spend gets half the credit. It acts on positive-advantage rollouts only and is rescaled to mean 1
     (credit moves between topics). A 4-step smoke run down-weighted 10-22% of rollouts from step 1.
   - The TODO in §III-B is gone. Check that R8 still reads right once the results are in.
2. **Interference Δ+ (N5).**
   - `PREREGISTRATION.md` lists it as a descriptive diagnostic, but no script computes it. Checkpoints are
     rolling, so the over-training curve cannot be rebuilt later.
   - The paper does not mention Δ+. Either add a probe before the main runs, or delete it from the
     pre-registration before the runs.
3. **The `[pilot]` values in `PREREGISTRATION.md`** (lr, steps, levels, partitioner). The paper picks these up
   automatically from the run configs.

## 9. Where the placeholder numbers come from

- **Source.** They come from `claude science/expected_results_forecast.md`:
  - vanilla GRPO raises Pass@1 by 5–7 pp and lowers Pass@32 by 4–8 pp;
  - the interaction is likely under-powered at 3 seeds.
- **Rescaling.** They are rescaled to MATH-500 using the Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct MATH score (~34%, Qwen2.5
  report) and smooth Beta-model Pass@k curves.
- **Ordering.** The ordering of conditions is the pre-registered *hypothesis*, not a result.
- **CIs.** The CI widths are fixed placeholders.

They exist only so the layout, tables and figures can be built before the data. They must all be gone (black)
before anyone outside the team sees the paper; `\finaltrue` enforces this.

## 10. Rules for the master copy

- One person regenerates `numbers.tex`. Everyone else edits prose in `main.tex`.
- Any change to the method or analysis must either match `PREREGISTRATION.md` or be added to its changelog and
  reported in the paper as a deviation.
- Claims follow the pre-registered rule: an effect is "significant" only if Holm p < 0.05 **and** the bootstrap
  CI excludes 0. Interactions (S1, S2) are always bounds, never verdicts. E1–E3 are always "exploratory".
- Cite Cue-GRPO as the source of the MEG rule. The contribution is the complementarity test, not the gate.
